## How it works

**Setup line** (small strip/callout at the top of the section):
"Text us, or hop on a 5-minute call. Give us their number. That's the whole setup."

**For them:**
- Every conversation adds to a real picture of them — not just today, but the pattern over weeks.
- A daily call or text, real conversation — not a script.
- Medications and appointments, followed through until it's actually done.
- Miss a check-in, and family hears about it right away.

**For you:**
- Ask anything, anytime — grounded in what Nelo's actually seen.
- Tell it what to check on next time, and it will.
- Get a recap by text or call — automatically every week, or on demand whenever you want one.
- Loop in your whole family, right from your group chat.

**Closing callout** (given its own visual weight after the two columns, not just a bullet — this is the memory/pattern-tracking moat):
"The longer it runs, the more it knows. Every check-in adds to the picture — mood, habits, the small things that repeat. Patterns show up clearly over weeks, in a way a once-a-week phone call never could."

## Images

Total: 2 images. Hero covers "For them," groupchat covers "For you" — no separate images inside the text columns, so neither side looks unfinished or lopsided.

**Image 1 — Hero (attention-grab + proof, in one)**
- Style: Maxi-style phone-in-hand shot, warm home background (not a cold city-skyline vibe — something that reads as family/domestic warmth).
- Content: phone lock screen with a notification bubble, e.g. "Nelo: Checked in with Mom — she's doing well, took her morning meds."
- Why: does double duty — grabs attention immediately, and the notification content itself is the concrete proof of "For them," so no separate "For them" image is needed later.
- Placement: hero section, large, centered/background, sits behind or beside the H1/H2.

**Image 2 — Groupchat (the "For you" proof, standalone)**
- Style: not a phone bezel — a cropped iMessage thread/card, flat, floating on the page. Deliberately different framing from the hero so it doesn't read as a repeat of the same image.
- Content: a family groupchat (something like "Mom's Care") with you, your sister, and Nelo in it — showing a moment like your sister asking Nelo something and getting a real answer, or you telling Nelo what to check on next time.
- Why: this is the actual differentiator — no competitor shows delegation across a family group. It proves "For you" concretely the same way image 1 proves "For them."
- Placement: standalone, full-width visual beat sitting after the "For them / For you" text columns — not squeezed into the columns themselves as a matching pair.
